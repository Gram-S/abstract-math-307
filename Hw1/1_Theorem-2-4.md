
*def 1.* Denote the set of integers: $\Z = \{..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...\}$.

*def 2.* An integer $n$ is odd if $n = 2k + 1 : k \in \Z$

*axiom 1.* Sums and products of integers must also be integers.

**Claim**: If $z \in \Z$ then the sum of $z$ and $z+1$ is odd.

<u>Proof</u>: 

Assume that $z \in \Z$.

Note that $1 \in \Z$, making it an integer by *def 1*.

Now, since both $1$ and $z$ are integers, their sum, $z+1$ is also an integer.

Thus, $z + (z+1)$ must be an integer.

We know that $z + z + 1 = 2z + 1$.

From *axiom 1.*, we know that $2z + 1 = n$, where $n$ is an odd integer.

Therefore, the sum of $z$ and $z+1$ must be odd.
