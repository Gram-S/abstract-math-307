*def 1.* Denote the set of integers: $\Z = \{..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...\}$.

*def 2.* An integer $n$ is even if $n = 2k : k \in \Z$.

*axiom 1.* Products of integers are integers.

**Claim**: If $n$ is an even integer then $n^2$ is an even integer.

<u>Proof</u>: 

Assume that $n$ is an even integer. 

Let $i$ be an integer.

For clarity, since $i$ and $k$ are both integers, let $i$ replace $k$ in *def 2* so that an even integer $n = 2i$.

Then, by *def 2*, $n^2 = n*n = 2i*2i = 4i^2$.

Note that, by *axiom 1*, $2i^2$ is an integer since both $2$ and $i$ are integers.

Therefore, $k = 2i^2$.

Now, observe that $n^2 = 4i^2 = 2 * 2i^2 = 2k$.

Since $n^2$ is equal to $2k$, by *def 2*, $n^2$ is an even integer.
