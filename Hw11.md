**Theorem 2.9**. The product of an odd integer and an odd integer is odd. 

**Theorem 2.10**. If at least one of a pair of integers is even then their product is even.



### Theorem 3.37

*"Assume that $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even"*. 

*Proof*.

The contrapositive of the claim is "If $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd." Thus we assume $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $n$ is an odd integer. By **Def 2.2** we know, $ n = 2k + 1 $ then $n(n) = n(2k + 1)$. Since both $n$ and $2k + 1$, by **Theorem 2.9**, their product is an odd integer. Thus $n^2$ is equal to an odd integer. Therefore, the contrapositive of our original claim is true. Hence, by **Theorem 3.34**, if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even. 

$\blacksquare$

### Theorem 3.38

*"Assume that $n, m \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $nm$ is odd, then both $n$ and $m$ are odd."*

*Proof*.

The contrapositive of our original claim is "If $n$ and $m$ are even, then $nm$ is even." Thus we assume that $n, m \in \mathbb{Z} $ and $n$ is even. Now, by **Theorem 2.10**, $nm$ is even. Therefore, the contrapositive of our original claim is true. Hence, by **Theorem 3.34**, if $nm$ is odd, then both $n$ and $m$ are odd. 

$\blacksquare$

### Theorem 3.39

*"Assume that $n, m \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $nm$ is even, then either $n$ or $m$ is even."*

*Proof*.

The contrapositive of our original claim is "If either $n$ or $m$ is odd, then $nm$ is odd." Thus we assume that $n, m \in \mathbb{Z}$ and the integers $n, m$ are odd. Now by **Theorem 2.9**, $nm$ is an odd integer. Therefore, the contrapositive of our original claim is true. Hence, by **Theorem 3.34**, if $nm$ is even, then either $n$ or $m$ is even. 

$\blacksquare$

### Theorem 3.41

*"Assume $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}.$ Then $15m + 35n \not= 1$."*

*Proof*.

Assume that $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$ but $15m + 35n = 1$ instead. Divide $5$ on each side of the equation to produce $3m + 7n = \frac{1}{5}$. However, by **Fact 2.3.2**, $3m + 7n$ must be an integer. Thus, assuming $15m + 35n = 1$ leads to a contradiction. Therefore, $15m + 35n \not= 1$ is true.

$\blacksquare$
