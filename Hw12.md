### Theorem 3.45

<i>"Suppose $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even."</i> <!-- Original claim -->

*Proof*.
<!-- begin -->

Assume that $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $n^2$ is even. For the sake of contradiction, assume that $n$ is odd. Then by **Def 2.1** and **2.2**, we know $(2k' + 1)^2 = 2k$. Now

$$
4k'^2 + 1 = 2k
$$
$$
4k'^2 = 2k - 1 
$$
$$
k = \frac{2k - 1}{4k'}.
$$

Recall that $k$ is an integer. But, $\frac{2k - 1}{4k'}$ is not an integer for any integers $k$, $k'$. Thus, assuming $n$ leads to a contradiction. Therefore, if $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $n^2$ is even, $n$ must also be even. 

$\blacksquare$

### Theorem 3.48

<i>"Products and sums of natural numbers are natural numbers."</i> <!-- Original claim -->

*Proof*.
<!-- begin -->

Assume $a, b \in \mathbb{N}$. For the sake of contradiction, assume that for some $v \notin \mathbb{N}$, then $a + b = v$ and $ab = v$. We know from the corollaries of **Def 2.1** and **2.2**, that the sum and product of an even and odd number must be either even or odd. Since $v$ cannot be a positive even or odd number, it must be a negative integer. But neither $a$ or $b$ are negative. Thus, assuming $a + b = v$ and $ab = v$ leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the products and sums of natural numbers are natural numbers. 

$\blacksquare$

### Theorem 3.49

<i>"Assume $x, y \in \mathbb{N}$. If $x$ divides $y$ then $x \leq y$."</i> <!-- Original claim -->

*Proof*.
<!-- begin -->
Assume $x, y \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x|y$. For the sake of contradiction, assume $x \gt y$. Now **Def 2.11* states $y = xk$. Then $\frac{y}{xk} = 1$. But, $xk$ is always strictly greater than $y$. So,  $\frac{y}{xk} = 1$ is a contradiction. Therefore, if $x, y \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x|y$, then $x \leq y$. 

$\blacksquare$
